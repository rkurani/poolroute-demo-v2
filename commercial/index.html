The problem

The pool is the one amenity nobody can see.

Commercial and HOA pools have to keep a daily chemistry log for the health department. On most properties that log is still a clipboard.

01 · Manual logs

The log lives on a clipboard

Readings are written by hand, often from memory, and filed where an inspector finds them before the manager does.

02 · Inconsistent testing

Every tech tests a little differently

Strip colors, kit technique and time of day change the number. A pool can drift out of range between visits and nobody knows.

03 · No visibility

The manager hears about it last

A closed pool, a resident complaint or a failed inspection is usually the first signal. There is no live view across properties.

DPD monitoring

A true free-chlorine reading, not an estimate.

The monitor measures free chlorine with the DPD method, the same chemistry as the test kit an inspector uses. ORP controllers infer chlorine from voltage, and that number drifts with pH and stabilizer.

  • Lab chemistry, on the poolFree chlorine and pH on a schedule you set, as often as every 15 minutes.
  • LTE built inNo Wi-Fi password, no hub, nothing on the property network. Enter the device's 10-digit ID and it goes live.
  • Automatic health-department logsEvery reading is written to the daily log with a timestamp. Print it or export it for an inspection.
  • Alerts and dosing recommendationsOut-of-range readings alert the manager and the on-call tech, with the exact dose to bring the pool back.
